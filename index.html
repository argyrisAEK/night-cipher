Night Cipher

A visual steganography game: solve hidden messages yourself or watch AI models hide and decode them.

How to play

Night Cipher has four ways to play: solve a hidden message yourself, or watch AI models compete in a hiding-and-decoding duel.

  1. Study the picture. The letters are hidden in something you can count or compare, like lit windows, bar heights, petals or dots. Look for a pattern rather than written text.
  2. In Learn and Daily, type your guess on the right. In AI vs AI Duel, you choose the secret message; AI Hider conceals it in an image, then AI Seeker tries to decode it. You watch the contest.
  3. Learn the trick. When the round ends, press “How was it hidden?” to see the method with the letters highlighted.

Four ways to play

  • 📅 Daily One puzzle a day, the same for everyone.
  • 🎓 Learn Ten levels, each with a different hiding method.

Classic LSB Steganography Lab

Hide UTF-8 text in the least-significant bits of RGB image channels. This is a local browser experiment: no AI calls or uploads are made.

How LSB encoding works
  1. Each red, green and blue channel is an 8-bit number from 0 to 255.
  2. The least-significant bit (LSB) is the final binary digit. Changing it alters a channel value by at most 1.
  3. This lab writes one message bit into each RGB channel, in row-major pixel order. Alpha values are left untouched.
  4. A small header identifies this lab's format and stores the UTF-8 message length. The header consumes 7 bytes of capacity.

Important: export and share the result as PNG. JPEG is lossy and may change the embedded bits. This simple educational encoder is not encrypted, authenticated, or designed to resist steganalysis.
